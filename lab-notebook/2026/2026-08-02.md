## Decisions

- **Validation stops at Tier 2 (ordinal / correlational / spatial) for v1.** A7's dimensionless
  readouts make absolute comparison impossible, and the nearest prior art did the same: Islam et al.
  (2023) matched dynamics and ranges, Cogno et al. (2022) matched shape and a severity index,
  Yoshizawa et al. (2022) matched spatial progression pattern. Absolute agreement is a post-hoc
  calibration exercise, not a v1 deliverable.
- **The focal-vs-diffuse experiment is scored with a spatial statistic, not an area fraction.**
  It holds total injury fixed, so a scalar burden cannot separate the conditions by construction.
  Ripley's K is the precedent (Roop et al., 2026). This also finally gives A14's focal→diffuse
  threshold an operational definition: the `S_M2` at which the collagen field becomes
  indistinguishable from a uniform random field.
- **Validate against the accumulation limb only**, i.e. the early and animal wood-smoke phase, not
  severe human COPD. v1 has no MMP arm and cannot produce the late collagen decrease.
- The spatial statistic is written as **post-processing on saved fields**, not as a steppable, so it
  can be re-run and re-tuned without re-running the simulation.
- **Ripley's K is the statistic**, scored by a *global* rank-based Monte-Carlo test rather than a
  pointwise envelope (see the bug below). Patch-size distribution and the mark-weighted variant are
  reported alongside it so the conclusion does not rest on one threshold choice.
- **The ECM field represents burden, not density** (A14). Burden is what Islam et al. (2023) and
  Brown et al. (2011) report as collagen area fraction, so the 2–40% band comparison stays valid,
  and it needs no per-site tissue-area denominator. Density is recoverable in post-processing.
- **EMT stays deferred, but now for a quantified reason rather than a vague one.** Rock et al.
  (2011) found no epithelium→myofibroblast conversion at all under lineage tracing; Tan et al.
  (2021) put the contribution at 1.94% of α-SMA⁺ cells. When v2 adds EMT it should be a low-rate
  term bounded near that figure, not a major route into the mesenchymal pool, and it must not
  displace `R_fib`.
- **Do not run the Roop et al. (2026) segmenter on model output.** Their code is public and the
  tempting move is to apply it to rendered CC3D frames. Rejected: the segmenter recovers collagen
  pixels from noisy stained images, and we already hold the field exactly, so it can only add
  error; it was trained on trichrome texture and would be far out of distribution on flat synthetic
  raster; and Ashcroft is scored on alveolar architecture, which the airway-wall domain does not
  have. The instrument-matching intuition is right but is satisfied by **matching the statistic,
  not the classifier** — same K estimator and edge correction on both sides. Their tool is instead
  the way to generate the *target*: run it on real biomass/wood-smoke sections and compare K curves.
  Written up as §3.7 of `docs/validation_targets.md`.
- **Radii are normalised by mean epithelial cell diameter** for any model-vs-histology comparison.
  Ripley's K is in units of distance and A7 forbids a lattice-to-µm mapping, so the two curves
  otherwise sit on different rulers. Normalising by a length measurable on both sides preserves A7.
  Verified exact (radii by `1/d`, `L − r` by `1/d`) and verified that the CSR p-value and the
  pass/fail verdict are invariant to it, so it cannot perturb the internal focal-vs-diffuse result.
- **Deferred deliberately:** the ODE reduction (`S_M2`, `R_fib`) and the CC3D v1 geometry. Tellurium
  will not install in the sandbox, so the reduction needs a host-side run in `tox-ode`; the geometry
  is better built after the reduction exists so it is not tested against placeholder constants.

## Results / observations

- **The literature does not agree on the sign of the collagen change in human COPD small airways,
  and this is the single most important thing to say before a reviewer says it.** Increased:
  Eurlings et al. (2014) at GOLD II, Kong et al. (2023) in bronchial submucosa (10.8% vs 3.8%).
  Decreased: Annoni et al. (2012) for type-I collagen, Higham et al. (2022), Polosukhin et al.
  (2021) in severe COPD, and Suzuki et al. (2010), who found small-airway collagen volume fraction
  *negatively* correlated with wall thickness. The resolution is stage and compartment, and it means
  our target is the animal wood-smoke phase.
- **Best validation target found is Hoshino et al. (1998), rs = 0.88** between subepithelial
  thickness and fibroblast number. It validates the *coupling* rather than an endpoint, it survives
  having no unit calibration, and it directly tests whether `R_fib` earns its place in the A15
  interface. If thickening reproduces with a fixed cell pool, `R_fib` should be dropped — a
  legitimate negative result.
- **Roop et al. (2026) is the methodological find.** Ripley's K on AI-segmented preclinical lung
  fibrosis separated samples with *similar Ashcroft scores and similar total fibrosis content* by
  spatial distribution alone. That is exactly our claim structure, from an independent field, which
  makes the focal-vs-diffuse experiment defensible as method and not just as a modelling curiosity.
- **Teitz-Tennenbaum et al. (2025)** separates two things we have been conflating: SO₂ exposure
  increased collagen fiber width, length and *number* but not fiber *density*. Worth deciding early
  whether our ECM field represents burden or density, because the model can report either.
- Eapen et al. (2021) remains the one human paper reporting the model's native normalisations
  (αSMA⁺ per mm² lamina propria and per mm Rbm), but the numbers we have are abstract-level only.
- `.gitignore` already carries the `*_cc3d_*/` backstop from the 07-30 next steps, confirmed by
  `git status` showing no untracked run dumps.
- **Two real bugs caught by the spatial-stats self-test, both of which would have produced a
  confidently wrong answer:**
  1. *Pointwise CSR envelope is the wrong test.* A pointwise 2.5/97.5 envelope has a 5% exceedance
     rate **at each radius**, so across ~20 radii a genuinely random field escapes it most of the
     time. My first run duly reported a uniformly random diffuse field as clustered. Replaced with
     a global max-absolute-deviation test, p = (1 + #{sim ≥ obs}) / (1 + n_sim). The diffuse control
     now returns p = 0.09 (not clustered), the focal control p = 0.01 (clustered).
  2. *Zero pooled standard error is a real case, not an error.* The burden-matched design can make
     every replicate land on exactly the same area fraction, which sent the Welch t to infinity and
     made the comparison report "arms are not burden-matched" for arms that were **perfectly**
     matched. Identical means with zero spread is now t = 0.
- The self-test is kept in the module and runnable as `--self-test`, including a null control (two
  diffuse arms must return FAIL, not invent separation) and an unmatched-burden control (must
  return INVALID, not PASS). Worth keeping because both bugs above were of the silent kind.
- Sandbox has **no package network**: `pip install` finds no index, so scipy and tellurium are both
  unavailable. The spatial-stats module was rewritten to numpy-only (FFT disc convolution instead
  of a KD-tree), which turned out to be the better dependency story anyway. The ODE reduction has no
  such workaround.
- **PMC full text is not reachable from here** (returns a CAPTCHA), so the Eapen/Higham/Kong number
  extraction is still open. The PubMed MCP connector was suggested but is not connected.
- **Three routes to automated Zotero entry are all closed**, worth recording so nobody retries them:
  the Connector's toolbar button is browser chrome and unreachable from page context; its
  `Ctrl+Shift+S` shortcut does not reach the extension (the keystroke landed in the page's search
  box); and the Zotero **web** library sits behind a login with a Cloudflare human-verification
  check. E-utilities in the browser works fine, which is why the identifier route was used instead.
- **Title searches returned the wrong paper three times out of nineteen** — roughly one in six — and
  every one looked plausible until checked:
  - "Origin of myofibroblasts in lung fibrosis" → PMID 16738204 = **Willis, duBois & Borok 2006**,
    *Epithelial origin of myofibroblasts during fibrosis in the lung*. Different paper, and
    ironically one that argues the opposite of `Rock2011`.
  - "Extracellular matrix composition in COPD" → the top two hits were **Lambers 2014** and
    **Kunz 2013**; the real `Annoni2012` was the *third* result (PMID 22496324), only visible after
    raising `retmax`.
  Lesson for next time: `esearch` alone is not sufficient evidence of a match. Always `efetch` and
  read back title, authors, journal and year before recording an identifier.
- `Li2024` returns nothing on a title search; the hyphenated "related-PM2.5" appears to break
  PubMed's title index. Four others (`Kong2023`, `Higham2022`, `Suzuki2010`, `Gunputh2026`) are
  conference abstracts or preprints and are simply not indexed.
