## Result: the challenge was aimed at the wrong layer

**The published ODE already predicts the chip's result.** Bennet et al. corroborate the signal
layer; they do not contradict it.

| ODE fact | Where |
|---|---|
| `E_d` is reversible and sub-lethal — repair `E_d → E_h` at `e4 = 0.002 h⁻¹` | Table 1; Eqs. 1–2; Table 2 |
| Damaged cells die at the same natural rate as healthy ones (`e2 = 0.001`) and still proliferate | Table 1 |
| `r = e3/(e2 + e4)` is defined as damage creation over removal by repair **or** natural death | stability analysis |
| Healthy epithelium secretes pro-inflammatory cytokine **under exposure**: `e5 = 0.1` vs `e6 = 0.2` damaged | Eq. 3, Table 2 |
| M2 and anti-inflammatory cytokine **enhance** repair: `e4(1 + e8(M2 + M2_R) + e10 A)` | Eq. 16 |
| Submodel validated on Calcabrini et al. (2004): A549, PM2.5, IL-6 and TNF-α over 72 h | §2.4.1 |

Nothing in the ODE's damaged state would release LDH. Its damaged:healthy secretion ratio is
**2:1**, gated by **exposure** rather than by damage, and the species is **pro-inflammatory `P`**,
not a growth factor. The subsystem was validated on cytokine release, not viability — the same
readout class Bennet et al. report.

So the 2026-10-11 reading "LDH is blind to the sub-lethal stressed state v1's *damaged* label
actually denotes" is confirmed by this project's own signal layer, not merely plausible. The other
two readings (sub-damage dose, too-steep ratio) are not refuted but are no longer needed.

**What is actually challenged are three tissue-layer departures from the ODE:**

1. **One-directional damage.** The ODE has `e4` plus M2-enhanced repair; v1 has neither. The tissue
   layer is more irreversible than the signal layer feeding it, and the published antioxidant
   intervention runs partly through the repair term v1 is missing.
2. **The 10:1 secretion ratio has no ODE provenance.** The ODE's is 2:1.
3. **The output species was swapped.** A19 has damaged epithelium producing the growth-factor field
   `G`; the ODE's epithelium produces pro-inflammatory `P`. A19 borrowed the ODE's damaged-state
   label and attached a different secretion to it. Defensible on Kumar 2004 / López-Guisa 2012 /
   Checa 2016, but it has to be argued on that evidence, not inherited.

## Decisions

- **Split A19** into **A19a** (pro-inflammatory secretion at ~2× the healthy exposed rate,
  ODE-anchored) and **A19b** (growth-factor secretion at an uncalibrated ratio, tissue-layer
  assumption, scanned parameter). Logged in `citation_todo.md`.
- **Add a repair path** for `EpitheliumDamaged`, rate from `e4` scaled to MCS (≈ 3.5e-5 per MCS per
  damaged cell at 62.4 s/MCS), and rerun the 20k baseline. This goes **ahead of** todo item 4,
  because it changes what every readout measures. Reopened as a block under `cc3d_v1_todo.md`
  Priority 2.
- **H1 re-scoped.** `f_d = 0` with the pedestal alone now tests whether the GF-from-damaged-
  epithelium channel (A19b) is necessary, not whether damage is.
- **H2 range corrected.** Scan the pro-inflammatory ratio around the ODE's 2:1, not 1:1 → 10:1.
- **New H5a, and it is the cheapest target in the project.** The ODE already emits `P`; the chip
  measures IL-6 and IL-8. Comparing the ODE's `P` trajectory against Bennet Fig. 6c needs no tissue
  code at all — it is the Calcabrini validation redone against a whole-smoke, ALI, co-culture
  dataset. Run it before anything else. Compare normalized shape and ordering only: their effluent
  is pooled, n = 3–7, and `P` is a lumped species rather than either named cytokine.
