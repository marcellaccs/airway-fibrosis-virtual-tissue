## Decisions
- **µm/px moves from 1.5625 to 1.25.** The uncited 10–15 µm / 12.5 µm midpoint describes large-airway
  pseudostratified columnar epithelium. A16 puts this model in the small airway, where the epithelium is
  simple cuboidal (`Plopper2020`). Three independent sources converge on 9–11 µm: 10.2 ± 1.0 µm for
  cultured human bronchial cells by planimetry (`Devalia1990`) and 9.3 µm for the Clara/secretory cell by
  TEM morphometry (`Mariassy1984`, within a 9.3–21.2 µm across-cell-type range). Taking 10 µm against
  `EPITHELIAL_CELL_WIDTH = 8` px gives 1.25 µm/px; the 200×100 px domain is 250 × 125 µm.
- **This is a reinterpretation, not a rescaling — no `J` or `lambdaVolume` change.** The pixel count per
  cell is unchanged, so Module 8.2's rescaling rule does not fire. Recorded explicitly in both docs so it
  is not re-litigated later.
- **The 40 µm/h dermal placeholder is retired.** `Tisler2020` gives a lung- and fibrosis-specific figure
  (below). The dermal number is 4–7× higher, so keeping it would have shortened the real time each MCS
  represents by the same factor.
- **GF-gated mitosis should gate location, not shorten the cycle.** In-culture doubling time is *not*
  reduced by fibrotic disease (`Mio1992`: 32.0 ± 6.0 h IPF vs 33.2 ± 10.4 h control, no difference) and is
  *lengthened* in emphysema (`Holz2004`: 33.6 ± 2.8 h vs 24.8 ± 1.4 h) — the exposure phenotype closest to
  this model. What is elevated in vivo is spatially localized clonal expansion (`Molina2025`). So the
  mitosis rule should decide where and whether a fibroblast divides, holding the cycle at ~25–34 h.
- **Item 4 (a second pixel anchor independent of cell width) is not closed, and fibroblast size will not
  close it.** Left open in `citation_todo.md` with the reason, and a better candidate named (in-tissue
  fibroblast profile area or RBM thickness from a section).

## Results / observations
- **Epithelial cell width.** `Devalia1990` 10.2 ± 1.0 µm (human bronchial, cultured, planimetry);
  `Mariassy1984` 9.3 µm Clara/secretory to 21.2 µm mucous M1, heights 9.6–30.6 µm (sheep tracheobronchial,
  TEM). Both are proxies — one human but cultured and large-airway, one in-situ but ovine. The
  *challenging* reading of `Mariassy1984` is that width spans >2× within a single epithelium, so fixing one
  width is choosing a cell type and the model should say which.
- **Fibroblast migration speed** (`Tisler2020`, primary human lung fibroblasts on image-based collagen
  scaffolds; 30-min imaging over 72 h; 100–200 cells/condition; ≥3 runs). Read from Fig. 3B *(verify)*:
  IPF cell / IPF matrix ≈ **10.7 µm/h** (SD ≈ 3.5); IPF / normal ≈ 9.8 (2.8); normal / IPF ≈ 7.3 (3.3);
  normal / normal ≈ 6.1 (2.1). IPF > normal cells P < 0.001; IPF > normal matrix P < 0.05.
- **The s/MCS solve is now writable.** `s/MCS = v[px/MCS] × 1.25 µm/px ÷ v_lit[µm/s]`; at 10.7 µm/h
  (2.97e-3 µm/s) that is **s/MCS ≈ 421 × v[px/MCS]**. Still needs the isolated-fibroblast scratch run to
  supply `v`.
- **The cell-cycle cross-check gives a hard ceiling.** ≥1000 MCS/cell-cycle at a 25–34 h cycle means
  **s/MCS ≤ ~90–120 s**. For reference `Chandrasegaran2025` runs at 27 s/MCS (a 30 h cycle = ~4000 MCS).
- **Consequence for the 2026-08-14 plateau finding.** At 27–120 s/MCS a 20,000 MCS run is 6–28 days of
  biological time — days, not the 24 weeks of `Li2024`. So "displacement plateaus by MCS 300–400" is a
  plateau within the **first few hours**, which strengthens the saturating-mechanism reading over a
  slow-progression one, and means the current run length is nowhere near the exposure timescale A11 scopes.
- **Challenging sources worth keeping visible.** `Asano2017`: migration is strongly stiffness-dependent
  (2 kPa ≪ 25 kPa), a variable v1 does not represent, so a single speed is a simplification.
  `Tisler2020` Discussion, citing Southern et al. (2016): on *ex vivo* bleomycin lung, migration was more
  *random*, the opposite of the scaffold result — the fibrotic-matrix speed-up is not established in real
  tissue.
- **Fibroblast spread area is not a usable second anchor.** `Asano2017` Fig. 2A *(verify)*: median cell
  projection area ≈ 800 µm² (1 kPa) rising to ≈ 2100 µm² (50 kPa). That is a flattened cell's full
  footprint on glass; `TARGET_VOLUME_FIB = 30` px = 46.9 µm² at 1.25 µm/px (equivalent diameter 7.7 µm) is
  a tissue *cross-section*, and the two are not comparable — 20–40× apart. Recorded rather than forced.
- **One consistency check the new numbers do enable.** `TARGET_VOLUME_EPITHELIAL = 48` px at 8 px width is
  6 px tall = 7.5 µm, against a measured Clara-cell height of 9.6 µm. 8×8 px (64 px) would match a cuboidal
  bronchiolar cell — but that one *does* change cell volume and so needs the `lambdaVolume` rescaling check.

## Did (continued, same thread — isolated-fibroblast run)
Built and ran the isolated-fibroblast scratch simulation the "Next steps" below originally called
for (`<scratchpad>/fibroblast_speed_calibration`, not committed — a throwaway calibration project,
same convention as the 2026-09-03 diagnostic scratch runs). One `Fibroblast` cell, no other cells,
no epithelium/boundary confinement, reusing the real model's own Potts/Contact/Chemotaxis/diffusion
parameters verbatim (`FluctuationAmplitude=10`, `ChemotaxisByType Lambda=1000`, field `G`
`GlobalDiffusionConstant=0.10`/`GlobalDecayConstant=0.01`, `Secretion Type="Fibroblast"=0.005`) so
the measurement reflects the real model's emergent motility, not a different parameterization.
Field `C` and every plugin/cell type a lone fibroblast can't reach (`ConnectivityGlobal`, FPP,
`SurfaceLocalFlex`, `OuterBoundary`, epithelium) were dropped rather than carried along unused.
Seeded at domain center (99.5, 49.5) in a 200×100 domain, 5000 MCS, `NoFlux` boundaries — tracked
`cell.xCOM`/`yCOM` every MCS and computed mean |ΔCOM| in `finish()`.

**Result: mean |ΔCOM| = 0.1484 px/MCS** (n=5000 samples). Max excursion from the start point was
only 12.65 px over the full run — the fibroblast never got closer than ~38 px to any `NoFlux` wall
(domain half-extents are 100×50 px), so the NoFlux boundary never biased the measurement.

**s/MCS solve, both `Tisler2020` anchors:**
- Conservative (normal cells on normal matrix, 6.1 µm/h): **s/MCS = 109.5** — under the ~120s
  ceiling, but only by ~9%, i.e. right at the edge of what Module 8.2's ≥1000 MCS/cell-cycle rule
  allows.
- Upper (IPF cells on IPF matrix, 10.7 µm/h): **s/MCS = 62.4** — comfortably under the ceiling,
  with real margin.

**Not yet decided: which anchor.** Both are defensible reads of `Tisler2020` and both clear the
ceiling, so this isn't blocked — but the choice isn't free of consequence either way (see Results
below for what each implies for run length in biological time), so it's flagged rather than picked
silently. This model's fibroblasts are undergoing wood-smoke/PM2.5-driven fibrotic activation, not
sitting at baseline, which leans toward the IPF/IPF anchor as the more representative population —
but that's an argument, not a decision, and the model's exposure (biomass/PM2.5) isn't the same
etiology `Tisler2020` used (IPF), so the lean is not strong. Left for the next session to close.

## Results (continued)
- **What either anchor makes a run mean, now with a measured number instead of the earlier
  27–120 s/MCS placeholder range:** a 20,000-MCS run is **14.4 days** of biological time at the
  IPF/IPF anchor (62.4 s/MCS) or **25.3 days** at the conservative anchor (109.5 s/MCS) — narrower
  than the earlier back-of-envelope 6–28 day estimate, and still nowhere near the 24 weeks `Li2024`
  and other exposure sources imply.
- **The 2026-08-14 plateau, now with a measured timestamp instead of an estimated one:** MCS
  ~300–400 (where lumen narrowing locks in) corresponds to **5.2–6.9 hours** of biological time at
  the IPF/IPF anchor, or **9.1–12.2 hours** at the conservative anchor. Either way, well under a
  day — reinforces the 2026-09-04 (earlier today) reading that this is a plateau within the first
  few hours, not a slow multi-week progression, and that the current run length falls far short of
  the exposure timescale A11 scopes regardless of which anchor is chosen.
