# Histology image credits — airway / small-airway fibrosis outreach talk

Compiled 2026-10-29 by Claude. All files live in `histology_images/` next to this file.
Every image below has a license confirmed on the source page (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 2.0, or
public domain). Nothing CC BY-NC / NC-ND was downloaded (see "Excluded" at the end).

Notes on use:
- CC BY-SA images: fine to show unmodified on slides/printouts with the attribution line.
  If you *annotate or crop* a CC BY-SA image and distribute the result, ShareAlike asks that
  the derivative carry the same CC BY-SA 2.0 license.
- Figure legends are quoted from the publisher XML (Europe PMC) or the Commons file page;
  anything I inferred by looking at the image rather than reading it on the page is marked
  "(not stated on source; my reading)".

## 1. `zou2014_fig2_small_airway_wall_thickening_control_vs_woodsmoke.png`
- **Shows:** Rat lung (female Sprague-Dawley rats per Methods), 7 months of whole-body exposure. Top row (a–c) lung parenchyma /
  airspace enlargement; bottom row (d–f) a small airway in cross-section with wall thickening —
  columns are Control, Cigarette smoke, Wood smoke. Insets are higher magnification. (g) mean
  linear intercept, (h) small-airway wall thickness bar charts at 4 and 7 months. Stain is H&E
  per the paper's Methods (the legend itself does not name the stain). Scale bar 50 µm.
  3445 × 2355 px.
- **Legend (verbatim):** "The airspace enlargement and small airway wall thickening in rats
  exposed to wood smoke. (a–c, g) Photomicrograph and graphical data demonstrating that the
  airspace size increased significantly in rats exposed to WS or CS for 7 months. (d–f, h)
  Photomicrograph and graphical data showing that the thickness of the small airway wall (SAW)
  increased significantly in rats exposed to WS or CS for 7 months. Data are shown as the
  mean ± SEM. n = 8 animals/group. Overall comparisons were made using one-way ANOVA (p<0.05).
  Scale bar = 50 µm."
- **Source:** Zou Y, Li S, Zou W, Hu G, Zhou Y, Peng G, He F, Li B, Ran P (2014). Upregulation
  of gelatinases and epithelial–mesenchymal transition in small airway remodeling associated
  with chronic exposure to wood smoke. *PLoS ONE* 9(5): e96708.
  https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0096708 — **Figure 2**.
  Image URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/figure/image?size=large&id=10.1371/journal.pone.0096708.g002
- **License:** CC BY 4.0 ("This is an open-access article distributed under the terms of the
  Creative Commons Attribution License...").
- **Attribution line:** Zou et al. 2014, *PLoS ONE* 9:e96708, Fig. 2, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0096708 (CC BY 4.0).

## 2. `zou2014_fig4_masson_trichrome_collagen_control_vs_woodsmoke.png`
- **Shows:** Same rat model, 7 months. Top row (a–c) Masson's trichrome — collagen stains blue
  around the small airway; bottom row (d–f) immunohistochemistry for type I collagen (brown);
  columns Control, Cigarette smoke, Wood smoke; insets at higher magnification. (g) area of
  Masson's trichrome staining and (h) area of type I collagen at 4 and 7 months. Scale bar
  50 µm. 3142 × 2156 px. This is the "collagen under the epithelium" picture.
- **Legend (verbatim):** "Increased collagen deposition in the small airways was induced by wood
  smoke. (a–f) Masson's Trichrome staining and IHC staining for Type I collagen showed
  increased collagen deposition in the small airway wall after 7 months of smoke exposure.
  (g, h) Graphical data showed that the area of total collagen and type I collagen deposition
  in the small airway wall exposed to WS or CS was significantly increased compared to
  controls at 7 months, but there was no significant difference at 4 months. Data are shown as
  the mean ± SEM or as box and whisker plots with the median, minimum and maximum values. n = 8
  animals/group. Scale bar = 50 µm."
- **Source:** Zou et al. 2014, *PLoS ONE* 9(5): e96708, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0096708
  — **Figure 4**.
  Image URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/figure/image?size=large&id=10.1371/journal.pone.0096708.g004
- **License:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Attribution line:** Zou et al. 2014, *PLoS ONE* 9:e96708, Fig. 4, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0096708 (CC BY 4.0).

## 3. `higham2019_fig2_COPD_bronchiole_masson_trichrome_and_HE.png`
- **Shows:** Human COPD small airways. (A) Masson's trichrome: a COPD bronchiole with a
  thickened, fibrotic wall — dense blue collagen bundles under the epithelium (10×).
  (B) H&E, bronchovascular bundle with a mucous plug in the lumen (red arrow, 2×). (C) H&E,
  goblet-cell hyperplasia in the epithelium (green arrows, 20×). (D) H&E, inflammatory cells in
  the bronchiole wall (black arrows, 20×). 1418 × 1004 px. Panel A is the best single
  "fibrotic small airway, collagen stain, human COPD" image in this set.
- **Legend (verbatim):** "Histopathological features of small airways disease in COPD. a A COPD
  bronchiole with a thickened airway wall due to fibrotic remodeling and excessive deposition
  of collagen bundles (blue colouration). Section stained with Masson's Trichrome (10X
  magnification). b A COPD bronchiolevascular bundle whereby the bronchiole contains a large
  intra-luminal mucous plug (red arrow) (2X magnification). Section stained with hematoxylin
  and eosin. c A COPD bronchiole with increased numbers of goblet cells (green arrows) in the
  epithelial lining (20X magnification). Section stained with hematoxylin and eosin. d The
  wall of a COPD bronchiole with increased numbers of inflammatory cells (black arrows) (20X
  magnification). Section stained with hematoxylin and eosin"
- **Source:** Higham A, Quinn AM, Cançado JED, Singh D (2019). The pathology of small airways
  disease in COPD: historical aspects and future directions. *Respiratory Research* 20:49.
  https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-019-1017-y — **Fig. 2**. (PMC6399904)
  Image URL: https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12931-019-1017-y/MediaObjects/12931_2019_1017_Fig2_HTML.png
- **License:** CC BY 4.0 ("This article is distributed under the terms of the Creative Commons
  Attribution 4.0 International License...").
- **Attribution line:** Higham et al. 2019, *Respir Res* 20:49, Fig. 2, doi:10.1186/s12931-019-1017-y (CC BY 4.0).

## 4. `hsieh2023_fig1_HE_nonasthmatic_vs_asthmatic_airway_subepithelial_fibrosis.png`
- **Shows:** Human conducting airway, H&E, side by side: left, non-asthmatic 11-year-old with no
  respiratory disease; right, fatal asthma, 15-year-old, with labelled arrows for angiogenesis,
  altered epithelial barrier, increased smooth muscle mass, mucus plug, thickened reticular
  basement membrane, goblet cell hyperplasia and **sub-epithelial fibrosis**. Scale bars 100 µm.
  1605 × 883 px. Asthma, not smoke exposure — included because it is the clearest openly
  licensed labelled picture of what "subepithelial fibrosis / airway wall remodelling" means.
  File note: the publisher serves this figure as WebP; I converted it to PNG with Pillow (no
  cropping, resizing or other change).
- **Legend (verbatim):** "Features of airway remodeling in asthma. Lung tissue was
  formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) and stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin stains. The
  left image in the panel demonstrates a conducting airway from a non-asthmatic individual (11
  years) with no history of respiratory disease. The right image demonstrates a conducting
  airway of a fatal asthmatic individual (15 years) showing features of airway remodeling
  including: an altered epithelial barrier with goblet cell hyperplasia, angiogenesis,
  increased smooth muscle mass, thickened reticular basement membrane, sub-epithelial fibrosis,
  and mucus plugging of the airway lumen."
- **Source:** Hsieh A, Assadinia N, Hackett T-L (2023). Airway remodeling heterogeneity in
  asthma and its relationship to disease outcomes. *Frontiers in Physiology* 14:1113100.
  https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1113100 — **Figure 1**. (PMC9892557)
  Image URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/1113100/xml-images/fphys-14-1113100-g001.webp
- **License:** CC BY ("This is an open-access article distributed under the terms of the
  Creative Commons Attribution License (CC BY)..."; Frontiers applies CC BY 4.0).
  Copyright © 2023 Hsieh, Assadinia and Hackett.
- **Attribution line:** Hsieh, Assadinia & Hackett 2023, *Front Physiol* 14:1113100, Fig. 1, doi:10.3389/fphys.2023.1113100 (CC BY 4.0).

## Excluded / could not get (and why)

- **Eapen MS et al. 2021, *ERJ Open Res* 7:00876-2020** (myofibroblasts in small airways of
  smokers/COPD; doi:10.1183/23120541.00876-2020). License is **CC BY-NC 4.0** — skipped as
  instructed.
- **Bennet TJ, Randhawa A, Pieters, Caffrey TM, Solomon T, ... Carlsten C, Cheung KC (2026).
  Engineering a multilayer microfluidic airway-on-a-chip with tunable GelMA hydrogel for
  physiologically relevant aerosol exposure studies. *Scientific Reports*,
  doi:10.1038/s41598-026-69468-5** (published 2026-09-09). The nature.com page states it is
  licensed **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** — skipped. The bioRxiv preprint (10.1101/2025.11.06.687056,
  posted 2025-11-08) is "All rights reserved. No reuse allowed without permission" — also
  unusable. If you want a chip/αSMA image from this work, you'd need to ask the authors
  (Cheung lab, UBC) for permission.
- **Hubbs AF et al. 2019, *Toxicol Pathol* 47:1012–1026 (diacetyl-injured mouse airway
  epithelium, H&E)** — exists on Commons as File:Popcorn Lung Hubbs crop.jpg tagged
  {{PD-USGov}} (NIOSH author), but the SAGE article is paywalled/blocked and not in PMC, so I
  could not verify the public-domain claim or the figure number against the source. Not kept.
- **Polosukhin et al. 2021, *Thorax*** (picrosirius red of COPD small-airway adventitia,
  PMC8526883) — BMJ open-access papers are usually CC BY-NC; not checked further.
- **Commons "Microscopic features of cystic fibrosis associated bronchiectasis"** (CC BY-SA
  2.0) — downloaded, then discarded: it shows granulomatous inflammation with a
  "@JPZhaoMD" watermark, not a clean peribronchiolar-fibrosis example.
- No picrosirius-red image of a smoke-exposed small airway under a CC BY license turned up in
  the searches; the Zou 2014 Masson's trichrome / type I collagen panels and the Higham 2019
  trichrome panel cover the collagen-stain need.
