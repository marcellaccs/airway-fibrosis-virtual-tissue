## Background & broader impacts (for the biological-background paragraph)

Global burden and the biomass/wood-smoke exposure link:

Biomass-smoke COPD is a distinct phenotype (greater airway involvement, less emphysema); inflammation + oxidative stress mechanism.

### Draft paragraph (biological background + broader impact)

> Repeated inhalation of wood-smoke particulate matter is a major global driver of chronic airway disease: household air pollution from solid fuels exposes roughly a third of the world's population and is a leading attributable risk factor for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma, with the burden concentrated in low- and middle-income settings [1][2][3]. Wood-smoke combustion products are qualitatively similar to tobacco smoke in particulate emissions and mechanism [4], yet biomass-associated COPD presents as a distinct phenotype with greater airway involvement, driven by inflammation and oxidative stress [5]. The step that converts reversible inflammation into fixed disease is structural: persistent injury drives subepithelial fibrosis and small-airway wall thickening, stiffening the airway and producing largely irreversible airflow limitation that current anti-inflammatory therapies do not reverse [6][7][8]. Because this remodeling is fundamentally a spatial reorganization of tissue architecture, identifying when it becomes self-sustaining, and which interventions interrupt it, is central to prevention and treatment.

## Assumption A1 — the anti/pro (A/P) or M2:M1 ratio drives fibroblast recruitment/activation

- **[9] [supports]** [Roles of Macrophage Polarization and Macrophage-Derived miRNAs in Pulmonary Fibrosis](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0f874e6378ab50f3bd6b7616241f7ea0/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Kishore A. & Petrek M., 2021, *Frontiers in Immunology*. M1/M2 phenotypes map to pro-inflammatory vs pro-fibrogenic signatures; the balance regulates fibrogenic mediator output.
- **[10] [supports]** [Cepharanthine attenuates pulmonary fibrosis via modulating macrophage M2 polarization](https://consensus.app/papers/details/502efaeb9d5c5d66a877ebd4a9b06299/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Bao J. et al., 2024, *BMC Pulmonary Medicine*. Reducing M2 polarization directly reduces fibroblast activation and fibrosis markers (causal, intervention-based).
- **[11] [challenges / nuances]** [Fibroblast-derived CXCL14 aggravates crystalline silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis by mediating polarization and recruitment of interstitial macrophages](https://consensus.app/papers/details/54717c2607b450189d006c121de37eb6/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — You Y. et al., 2023, *J. Hazardous Materials*. Causality runs both ways: activated fibroblasts recruit and polarize macrophages. A one-directional "ratio → fibroblast" signal is a simplification of a feedback loop.

Reviewer note: the literature supports M2 dominance being pro-fibrotic directionally, but no paper establishes a specific numeric A/P (or M2:M1) *threshold* that recruits fibroblasts. Treat the ratio-as-master-signal as a modeling posit, and cite [9][10] for direction, [11] for the feedback caveat.

## Assumption A2 — TGF-β is the dominant profibrotic M2 signal

- **[12] [supports]** [Transforming growth factor-β signaling: from tissue fibrosis to therapeutic opportunities](https://consensus.app/papers/details/57e0e714ddee51efb395f8aeec6d3679/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Ren L.-L. et al., 2022, *Chemico-Biological Interactions*. TGF-β as central driver of fibroblast proliferation, myofibroblast generation, ECM deposition, and collagenolysis inhibition.
- **[13] [supports, airway-specific]** [Enhanced asthma-related fibroblast to myofibroblast transition is the result of profibrotic TGF-β/Smad2/3 intensification and antifibrotic TGF-β/Smad1/5/9 impairment](https://consensus.app/papers/details/dde8d02632eb50e9a57fe56a053b1b0b/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Wnuk D. et al., 2020, *Scientific Reports*. TGF-β1 drives FMT and subepithelial fibrosis specifically in human bronchial fibroblasts (directly on-target for small airway).
- **[14] [challenges]** [Leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) receptor amplifies pathogenic activation of fibroblasts in lung fibrosis](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0e19b9a0aacd5ab7b1e0491b2c029c53/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Nguyen H.N. et al., 2024, *PNAS*. Clinical trials targeting TGF-β alone "largely failed"; IL-4, IL-13, and a LIFR amplifier co-drive fibroblasts. TGF-β is necessary but not solely sufficient — supports keeping the other M2 mediators (PDGF, IL-4/13) in the model, per your own design notes.

## Assumption A6 — a persistent Type II signal alone is sufficient to generate subepithelial fibrosis

- **[15] [supports]** [Fibrosis in PCLS: comparing TGF-β and fibrotic cocktail](https://consensus.app/papers/details/eaf742039a5b555d92b43334c82006d7/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Machahua C. et al., 2025, *Respiratory Research*. In human lung slices, TGF-β1 alone induces profibrotic ECM/myofibroblast changes comparable to (or exceeding) a full pro-fibrotic + pro-inflammatory cocktail — the strongest single-signal-sufficiency evidence.
- **[14] [challenges]** Nguyen et al., 2024, *PNAS* (as above). Single-pathway sufficiency does not hold in vivo/clinically; multiple upstream activators sustain fibroblasts.
- **[6] [context]** Savin et al., 2023, *IJMS* (as above). Frames fibrosis as the end of a persistent-inflammation → remodeling continuum rather than a single-signal switch.

Reviewer note: defensible as an *in-model* sufficiency claim (cite [15]); flag [14] as the reason spatial/in-vivo sufficiency is contested.

## Assumption A4 — antioxidant intervention limits ECM deposition / slows remodeling

- **[16] [supports, review]** [The Role of Nrf2 in Pulmonary Fibrosis: Molecular Mechanisms and Treatment Approaches](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6defb33c8fa159f09ed9390a472ad4a7/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Wang Y. et al., 2022, *Antioxidants*. Nrf2 activators attenuate fibrosis by targeting oxidative stress, fibroblast-myofibroblast differentiation, and EMT.
- **[17] [supports, mechanistic]** [ROS-responsive liposomes as an inhaled drug delivery nanoplatform for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis via Nrf2 signaling](https://consensus.app/papers/details/15dfba2159f65ef087cc28d120e7e2f6/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Liu J. et al., 2022, *J. Nanobiotechnology*. Antioxidant (Nrf2) activation reduces macrophage-mediated fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition and ECM deposition — mirrors your ODE→tissue causal chain.
- **[18] [supports, review]** [Roles of NRF2 in Fibrotic Diseases: From Mechanisms to Therapeutic Approaches](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9b5d0cbcb47a5f9f8d9a2e4038f9bd80/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Hao W. et al., 2022, *Frontiers in Physiology*. Cross-organ evidence that antioxidant (KEAP1/NRF2) modulation reduces myofibroblast ECM output.

Reviewer note: strong biological grounding for the *direction* of the antioxidant effect; the mapping of "antioxidant" to specific ODE/CC3D parameters remains a modeling choice to state explicitly.

## Assumption A5 — wood-smoke exposure as a Boolean on/off switch (no dose–response)

- **[19] [partial support]** [Screening protocol to identify persons responsive to wood smoke particle-induced airway inflammation](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ac20b8f5900b59a8a849aefb265d2bdb/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Alexis N.E. et al., 2022, *Inhalation Toxicology*. Controlled 500 µg/m³ WSP triggers neutrophilic airway inflammation with responder/non-responder classes — consistent with threshold-like behavior but with strong inter-individual variability.
- **[20] [supports, model-relevant]** [Short-Term Exposure to Wood Smoke Increases Pro-Inflammatory Cytokines, Gelatinases, and TIMPs in Guinea Pigs](https://consensus.app/papers/details/88101e1f8020591294afa0ac38bddc67/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Ramos C. et al., 2021, *Toxics*. Wood-smoke dose upregulates cytokines, MMP-2/9, TIMP-1/2, and collagen — directly validates the model's cytokine/MMP/TIMP/ECM variables.
- **[21] [challenges]** [Reduced bronchoalveolar macrophage phagocytosis and cytotoxic effects after controlled short-term wood smoke exposure](https://consensus.app/papers/details/e6d28ed4f71159439f30bea5eb8b9733/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Hansson A. et al., 2023, *Particle and Fibre Toxicology*. At 409 µg/m³, wood smoke produced cytotoxicity and impaired macrophage phagocytosis but *no* acute inflammatory cell influx — the response is more nuanced than "PM on → inflammation on," arguing against a pure Boolean switch.

Reviewer note: the Boolean switch is defensible as a first approximation (cite [19][20] for threshold + ECM relevance), but [21] shows dose/kinetics and cytotoxic-vs-inflammatory distinctions the switch omits — worth acknowledging as a known simplification.

## Assumption A19 — damaged epithelial cells secrete growth factor at a higher rate than healthy epithelial cells

*Added 2026-08-09. This assumption postdates the 2026-07-22 review; it is stated in full in `docs/model_assumptions_for_slides.md` (A19) and underpins A14's stationary source and the focal-vs-diffuse experiment.*

- **[22] [supports]** [Expression of growth factors by airway epithelial cells in a model of chronic asthma: regulation and relationship to subepithelial fibrosis](https://consensus.app/papers/details/14ba91c4dcdf5c8cb7553206f4a75288/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Kumar R.K. et al., 2004, *Clinical and Experimental Allergy*. Airway epithelial expression of the cleaved, potentially bioactive TGF-β1 rose with allergen challenge, and its accumulation was related to airway-wall remodeling; eosinophils were *not* a significant TGF-β1 source, isolating the epithelium as the relevant stationary source.
- **[23] [supports, healthy-vs-diseased at the cell level]** [Airway epithelial cells from asthmatic children differentially express proremodeling factors](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d127d76dbf4257bfaa1ca7d6931d5e81/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — López-Guisa J.M. et al., 2012, *Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology*. Bronchial and nasal epithelial cells from asthmatic children (n = 16) produced significantly more TGF-β2 and VEGF and expressed ~3.7-fold more periostin than cells from healthy children (n = 15) after 3 weeks at air–liquid interface. The difference survives removal from the airway, so it is a property of the epithelial cells, not the milieu — the closest available analogue of a two-cell-type secretion-rate difference.
- **[24] [supports, exposure-specific]** [Cigarette smoke enhances the expression of profibrotic molecules in alveolar epithelial cells](https://consensus.app/papers/details/42a4621a93965f68adb8b816050855aa/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Checa M. et al., 2016, *PLOS ONE*. Cigarette-smoke-extract exposure upregulated >1000 genes including TGF-β and Wnt pathways and increased secretion of *activated* TGF-β1 versus unexposed controls, with the increase integrin-αv-dependent. Nearest available proxy for wood-smoke PM, and it speaks to A14's latent-vs-active distinction as well as to A19.
- **[25] [supports, causal / in vivo]** [Activated alveolar epithelial cells initiate fibrosis through autocrine and paracrine secretion of connective tissue growth factor](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edebedd0ac33594e9eefd40d4d65fd1f/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Yang J. et al., 2014, *American Journal of Physiology: Lung Cellular and Molecular Physiology*. Epithelial CTGF promoter activity rose after bleomycin in vivo, and lung-epithelial-specific CTGF deletion attenuated the fibrotic response — epithelium-derived growth factor is load-bearing, not incidental.
- **[26] [supports, particulate]** [Epithelial 3D-spheroids as a tool to study air pollutant-induced lung pathology](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5d5e497e9d8c521b9e56aea0cd9638f5/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Baarsma H.A. et al., 2022, *SLAS Discovery*. Diesel exhaust particles increased TGF-β1 protein in bronchial epithelial cells and induced an EMT-like phenotype in 3D spheroids; cigarette smoke likewise activated TGF-β1 release and signaling. Particulate exposure, bronchial (not alveolar) epithelium.
- **[27] [challenges / nuances]** [Bidirectional epithelial–mesenchymal crosstalk provides self-sustaining profibrotic signals in pulmonary fibrosis](https://consensus.app/papers/details/94a3f0eac7175880a353e82faffb2e4b/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Yao L. et al., 2021, *Journal of Biological Chemistry*. Paracrine signalling from TGF-β-activated or IPF fibroblasts reactivates RAS in alveolar epithelial cells via SPARC/EGFR, so epithelium-to-fibroblast signalling is one arm of a closed loop. Same caveat A1 carries via You et al. (2023) [11]; v1 omits the return arm.
- **[28] [challenges]** [Cigarette smoke impairs airway epithelial wound repair: role of modulation of EMT processes and Notch-1 signaling](https://consensus.app/papers/details/992b98ef32c05432bfa886a85327b9d8/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Di Vincenzo S. et al., 2022, *Antioxidants*. In primary bronchial epithelial cells, cigarette smoke *decreased* wound repair, vimentin, Notch-1/JAGGED1/HES1, MMP9 and TP63, and counteracted TGF-β1's effects. Injury is not a uniform upregulation of profibrotic output — "damaged ⇒ secretes more" is directionally right for TGF-β but wrong as a blanket statement about the damaged phenotype.
- **[29] [challenges]** [The role of WNT5a and TGF-β1 in airway remodelling and severe asthma](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1a61701d2f3452818e3450d42081d797/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Daud T. et al., 2025, *Allergy*. Epithelial WNT5a correlated positively with % intact epithelium (rs = 0.54) and negatively with % denuded epithelium (rs = −0.39): shed epithelium cannot secrete. v1's damaged cells persist and keep secreting, which is defensible for sublethal chronic exposure but would misrepresent frank denudation.
- **[30] [challenges, exposure-specific]** [The role of transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/bbeb42949de456afaab247d1aec0d9fd/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Kraik K. et al., 2024, *Cells*. TGF-β's role in COPD is ambiguous: initially protective (tissue repair, inflammation restraint), turning profibrotic only once prolonged exposure dysregulates the signal. A19's direction holds within the persistent-exposure regime scoped by A5 and A11, not universally.

Reviewer note: the *direction* is strongly supported — including by a like-for-like healthy-vs-diseased epithelial cell comparison [23] and a causal in-vivo knockout [25] — but **no source supplies a calibrated damaged:healthy secretion ratio** for small-airway epithelium. Treat the ratio as a scanned parameter with a non-zero healthy floor (per A7's dimensionless convention), cite [22]–[26] for direction, and name [27]–[30] as the omitted return arm, the non-monotonicity of "damage," and the exposure-regime caveat.

## Assumption A7 — scale calibration: the pixel↔µm and MCS↔time anchors

*Added 2026-09-04. A7 originally deferred scale calibration ("no committed mapping of lattice site to µm or MCS to hours"); the 2026-08-14 note reopened it. These are the literature anchors for the two conversion factors. Numbers marked **(verify)** were read off a published figure rather than a table — see the reviewer note.*

**Anchor 1 — airway epithelial cell width (sets µm/px).**

- **[31] [supports]** [Culture and comparison of human bronchial and nasal epithelial cells in vitro](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2e9390dbcf565eb282108f5135eafd28/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Devalia J.L. et al., 1990, *Respiratory Medicine*. Planimetry of confluent primary cultures gave **mean cell width 10.2 ± 1.0 µm (bronchial)** and 10.6 ± 1.1 µm (nasal); 50–76% of cells were mature ciliated type at early passage. Human, but cultured and large-airway — a proxy for bronchiolar epithelium in situ.
- **[32] [supports, independent method]** [Tracheobronchial epithelium of the sheep: II. Ultrastructural and morphometric analysis of the epithelial secretory cell types](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4be216d7bfef5f67971583daf3422a61/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Mariassy A.T. & Plopper C.G., 1984, *The Anatomical Record*. TEM morphometry of six granule-containing cell types: **cell width ranged 9.3 µm (Clara/secretory) to 21.2 µm (mucous M1)**; cell height 9.6 µm (Clara) to 30.6 µm (M1). The Clara-cell figure is the closest match to the secretory cell type that dominates small-airway epithelium; species (sheep) and level (tracheobronchial) are both proxies.
- **[33] [supports, qualitative]** [Structural Methods for Studying Bronchiolar Epithelial Cells](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6fae9d1af1a8502180cdb9ca205b7d0e/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Plopper C.G., 2020, *Lung Biology in Health and Disease*. Bronchioles are lined by **simple cuboidal** epithelium of nonciliated secretory and ciliated cells — not the tall pseudostratified columnar epithelium of the large airways. Independent qualitative reason to expect small-airway cells at the low end of any generic "airway epithelial cell" width range.
- **[34] [challenges]** Same source as [32]. Within one epithelium, cell width spans **more than a factor of two** (9.3–21.2 µm) across cell types. There is no single "airway epithelial cell width"; the number depends on which cell type and which airway generation is meant. A model that fixes one width is choosing a cell type, and should say which.

**Anchor 2 — fibroblast migration speed (sets s/MCS).**

- **[35] [supports, lung- and fibrosis-specific]** [Analysis of fibroblast migration dynamics in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis using image-based scaffolds of the lung extracellular matrix](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6aad8a2308645798a7851386c3c8a03a/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Tisler M. et al., 2020, *Am J Physiol Lung Cell Mol Physiol* 318:L276–L286. Primary human lung fibroblasts on MPE-fabricated collagen scaffolds derived from SHG images of normal and IPF lung; phase-contrast imaging every 30 min over 72 h, 100–200 cells per combination, ≥3 independent experiments. **Average speed (µm/h), read from Fig. 3B *(verify)*: IPF fibroblast on IPF matrix ≈ 10.7 (SD ≈ 3.5); IPF on normal ≈ 9.8 (SD ≈ 2.8); normal (nHLF) on IPF ≈ 7.3 (SD ≈ 3.3); nHLF on normal ≈ 6.1 (SD ≈ 2.1).** IPF cells faster than normal cells on both matrices (P < 0.001); both cell types faster on the aligned IPF matrix (P < 0.05).
- **[36] [supports, direction only]** [Enhanced migration of fibroblasts derived from lungs with fibrotic lesions](https://consensus.app/papers/details/13a0014648825f9ba135c6a90890b000/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Suganuma H. et al., 1995, *Thorax*. Boyden chamber, cells/field: control 183 (86) vs IPF 689 (491) in serum-free medium; 829 (222) vs 1928 (600) with PDGF. Fibroblasts from denser fibrosis migrated more than those from earlier-stage lesions, and migratory activity did **not** correlate with proliferative capacity — relevant to keeping the motility and mitosis parameters independent.
- **[37] [challenges, magnitude is context-dependent]** [Matrix stiffness regulates migration of human lung fibroblasts](https://consensus.app/papers/details/60eaaaf27439522994df55ddb1750040/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Asano S. et al., 2017, *Physiological Reports* 5(9):e13281. Random-walk migration of normal human lung fibroblasts precultured on 25 kPa gel or plastic was significantly greater than on 2 kPa gel; α-SMA knockdown reduced it. Reported as total distance over 12 h in box plots, so it constrains **direction, not magnitude** — and it shows a single "fibroblast speed" is a function of substrate stiffness, which the v1 model does not represent.
- **[38] [challenges]** Tisler et al. [35] Discussion, on Southern et al. (2016): migration on *ex vivo* bleomycin-fibrotic mouse lung was **more random**, not more directed — the opposite of the contact-guidance result on fabricated scaffolds. Both stiffness and morphology differ between the two systems, so the fibrotic-matrix speed-up should not be treated as established in real tissue.
- **[39] [challenges, tissue of origin]** Chandrasegaran S. et al., 2025, *PLOS Comput Biol* 21(4):e1012298 (`Chandrasegaran2025`, in `literature/cc3d_lit`). The CC3D precedent this project has been using as a fallback calibrates against a **dermal** fibroblast speed of 40 µm/h — roughly **4–7× the lung values in [35]**. Tissue of origin is not a detail here: it changes the s/MCS solve by the same factor.

**Anchor 3 — fibroblast doubling time (mitosis rate, and the MCS↔time cross-check).**

- **[40] [supports, in vivo]** [Clonal expansion of alveolar fibroblast progeny drives pulmonary fibrosis in mouse models](https://consensus.app/papers/details/599666e3f09a52b7b49ed9ae41c52dcc/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Molina C. et al., 2025, *Journal of Clinical Investigation*. EdU incorporation, lineage tracing and scRNA-seq across two PF models: substantial DNA replication in alveolar-fibroblast progeny, with clonal expansion **principally in regions of fibrotic remodeling**; ablating proliferating fibroblasts mitigated fibrosis. Fibroblasts in precision-cut slices from human fibrotic lungs proliferated more than nondiseased. Direct support for adding proliferation, and for gating it **spatially** rather than raising a global rate.
- **[41] [challenges]** [Proliferative characteristics of fibroblast lines derived from open lung biopsy specimens of patients with IPF (UIP)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/db6d8080166d57628e3d8f785c11da16/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Mio T. et al., 1992, *Chest*. **Doubling time 32.0 ± 6.0 h (IPF/UIP, n = 10 lines) vs 33.2 ± 10.4 h (control, n = 10) — no difference**, and no difference between higher- and lower-intensity fibrotic lesions. The intrinsic cell cycle is not shortened by disease.
- **[42] [challenges, and it is the COPD-relevant one]** [Lung fibroblasts from patients with emphysema show a reduced proliferation rate in culture](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fcfdabcfc7e154b3bbd61da54f1ed9ae/?utm_source=claude_desktop) — Holz O. et al., 2004, *European Respiratory Journal*. **Doubling time 33.6 ± 2.8 h (emphysema, median FEV1 40% pred., n = 10) vs 24.8 ± 1.4 h (control, n = 10)**; maximum population doubling level 13.8 (7.4–22.6) vs 20.2 (11.2–25.5), not significant. In the COPD phenotype this model is closest to, lung fibroblasts proliferate **more slowly**, which the authors read as an intrinsic failure of cellular replacement.

**Reviewer note.**

1. *The width anchors converge below the value in use.* [31], [32] and [33] all put a bronchiolar/secretory airway epithelial cell at roughly **9–11 µm**, not at the 12.5 µm midpoint of the 10–15 µm range the model has been carrying. 10–15 µm describes large-airway pseudostratified columnar epithelium; A16 places the model in the small airway. Using ~10 µm gives **1.25 µm/px** rather than 1.5625.
2. *No rescaling of `J` or `lambdaVolume` follows.* This changes the *interpretation* of the lattice, not its resolution — `EPITHELIAL_CELL_WIDTH` stays 8 px. Module 8.2's rescaling rule applies when the pixel/µm ratio changes *for a fixed physical cell size*; here the pixel count per cell is unchanged.
3. *The migration numbers in [35] are figure reads, not tabulated values.* Fig. 3B has a labelled µm/h axis and the values are unambiguous to ±0.5 µm/h, but they must be confirmed against the source figure (or requested from the authors) before appearing in a manuscript. Marked *(verify)* in `docs/validation_targets.md`.
4. *Dropping the dermal fallback changes the answer by 4–7×.* See [39]. This is the single largest correction available to the MCS↔time solve.
5. *Proliferation should be gated, not accelerated.* [40] vs [41]/[42] reconcile as: in-vivo proliferation is elevated and **spatially localized**, while the intrinsic cycle time is not shortened (and is *lengthened* in emphysema). So GF-gated mitosis should decide *where and whether* a fibroblast divides, with the cycle time held at the measured **~25–34 h**; raising a global division rate would contradict [41] and [42].
6. *No source supplies an in-tissue fibroblast cross-sectional area,* so the pixel↔µm calibration still rests on the epithelial-width anchor alone. The available fibroblast-size literature reports **2D spread area on a substrate** ([37], Fig. 2A: median ≈ 800–2100 µm² across 1–50 kPa *(verify)*), which is a cell's full footprint when flattened on glass — not its profile in a tissue cross-section, and 20–40× larger. Logged as still open in `docs/citation_todo.md`.

**Anchor 4 — a second, independent pixel↔µm anchor (added 2026-09-07).**

The 2026-09-04 pass left the calibration resting on epithelial width alone, having ruled out fibroblast

**Reviewer note, 2026-11-01.**

16. *Same free D, different dressing.* The "same molecular size ⇒ same D" argument is correct for free solution [78, 79] and wrong for tissue: the effective D is set by binding, and the three molecules bind differently — TGF-β1 essentially irreversibly to ECM [85, 86], IL-8 strongly to HS/CS in lung [95, 96], IL-6 only partially [98]. The expected ordering of effective ranges is therefore **λ_G ≲ λ_IL-8 < λ_IL-6**, which the measured figures match: ~70 µm or less for TGF-β1 [90–93] against 80–120 µm for cytokines in vivo [57].
17. *The current shared D = 0.10 px²/MCS puts both fields below their ranges.* With k_G = 0.01 /MCS it gives λ_G = 3.2 px = **4 µm**, under one cell width; with k_P = 6.93e-4 /MCS it gives λ_P = 12 px = **15 µm**. Only the zero-range reading of TGF-β1 [92, 93] and the dense-consumer/GAG-immobilised reading of IL-8 [56, 95] defend those values. They should be the **lower sensitivity arms**, not the baseline.
18. *A shared D is compatible with the literature if it is raised, not if it stays.* Equal D with the model's two decays fixes λ_P/λ_G = √(k_G/k_P) = 3.8, inside the 1–10 spread the sources allow. Shared **D = 2.5–4.5 px²/MCS** gives λ_G = 20–26 µm and λ_P = 76–100 µm, both inside range; the 2026-10-30 `P` pin (4.44) already sits in that window. So the one-knob rule can stay, but the knob has to move ~25–45×.
19. *`G` decay is bounded, not pinned.* No tissue half-life for active TGF-β1 exists. Plasma gives 1.6–3 min [87, 88] (k = 0.24–0.45 /MCS); heparin protection triples it [86] (~5–9 min, k ≈ 0.08); latent complex > 100 min [87] (k < 0.0072). The current 0.01 /MCS (t½ 72 min) sits at the latent end, which is defensible only if `G` is read as the lumped active + latent pool (A2's "TGF-β-like" wording). At any faster decay the D needed for a 20 µm range becomes 20–100 px²/MCS and the solver cost dominates; pin λ and keep k = 0.01, stating this.
20. *What would close it.* Ross & Pompano's micro-IOI [79] on a lung slice, or Daher et al.'s transcriptomics calibration [94] applied to a small-airway dataset, would give a measured effective D for either field. Neither exists for airway tissue; both are logged in `citation_todo.md`.
